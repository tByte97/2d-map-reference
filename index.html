How the detective decides

Season averages. Each year gets one number per spot: the average of its months in the chosen season (winter = December of the year before + January + February).

Rate: Sen's slope. The median of the slopes between every pair of years. One freak year can't drag it, unlike a straight-line fit. The 95% range comes from the same pairs.

Proof: Mann–Kendall test. Counts how often later years beat earlier ones. p is the chance of seeing a pattern at least this steady if nothing were really changing. Below 0.05 we call it a trend, below 0.01 proven.

Honest about memory. A wet year often follows a wet year, which makes trends look surer than they are. We measure that year-to-year memory (r₁) and widen the test to match.

Chance on the map. Test 5,000 spots and about 5% look significant by luck alone. The map caption compares the share of significant spots to that 5%.

Limits. MERRA-2 is a reanalysis: a weather model steered by observations, ~50 km cells. Sunlight starts in 1984. Snow depth and soil moisture are modelled, not measured directly.
